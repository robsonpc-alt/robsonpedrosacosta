Research

Slavery, gender, Africa & the Atlantic World

My research combines connected, comparative, and transnational approaches to slavery, freedom, gender, religion, and social hierarchy across Brazil, Africa, and the diaspora.

Current primary focus

Women, Slavery & African Diaspora

My current research focuses especially on the histories and experiences of African and Afro-descendant women. I examine slavery, resistance, freedom, motherhood, family, mobility, religion, and the multiple connections linking Africa and the diaspora. This agenda is developed through the CNPq-funded project Women of Africa, Women of the Diaspora: Connections, Transgressions and Resistance, international research networks, conferences, and publications.

Long-term research

Religion, Slavery & Resistance

A second major body of work explores the role of religious institutions in the creation, management, and legitimation of slavery, especially the Benedictine Order in Brazil. This research investigates paternalism, moral regulation, emancipation, family formation, institutional power, and the strategies through which enslaved people negotiated or resisted domination.

The project SLAVESRELIGION – Slaves of Religion: Paternalism and Resistance, Brazil was developed during a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Postdoctoral Fellowship at the University of Lisbon (2022–2024) and continues in a second phase.

Specialized research agenda

Slave-owning Slaves

This strand examines enslaved men and women who themselves owned enslaved people. By tracing individuals and comparing cases in Brazil and Africa, the research questions rigid assumptions about status and hierarchy and explores social mobility, dependency, property, kinship, and the internal complexities of slave societies.

Key publications

“Nicolau de Souza: the trajectory of a slave-owning slave, Brazil, 1812–1835” (2021); “Black Masters: A Study on Slave-owning Slaves” (Brill, 2024); two forthcoming articles in Revista de Indias and Topoi (2026).

Comparative direction

Current writing extends this agenda from Brazil toward comparative and connected perspectives on slave-owning slaves in Africa and the Atlantic world.
