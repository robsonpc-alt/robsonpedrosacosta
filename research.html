Research

Research

Connected and comparative histories of slavery, women, religion, resistance, and dependency across Africa, Brazil, and the Atlantic World.

◉
Current priority

Women, Slavery & African Diaspora

Research on African and Afro-descendant women and the connected histories that link Africa, Brazil, Latin America, the Caribbean, and the wider diaspora. Themes include slavery, resistance, freedom, motherhood, family, religion, mobility, and political agency.

✦
Long-term research

Religion, Slavery & Resistance

Studies on Catholic institutions as slaveholders—especially the Benedictine Order—paternalism, emancipation, negotiation, moral regulation, and resistance in Brazil and beyond.

∞
Specialized agenda

Slave-owning Slaves in Africa and Brazil

Investigations into enslaved individuals who themselves owned enslaved people: hierarchy, legal status, agency, dependence, social mobility, and challenges to dominant interpretations of slavery.

◎
Methodological approach

Connected, comparative & transnational history

Microhistorical sensitivity combined with connected and comparative perspectives to examine how slavery, gender, religion, and dependency changed across different societies and historical contexts.

“Exploring the past to understand the present and connect worlds.”
