Academic profile

Robson Pedrosa Costa is a historian specializing in slavery, gender, and the Atlantic World, with particular interests in Brazil, Africa, and the African diaspora. His current research focuses especially on the histories and experiences of African and Afro-descendant women, examining slavery, resistance, freedom, motherhood, family, mobility, religion, and the multiple connections linking Africa and the diaspora.

His broader scholarship also addresses institutional slavery, religion, paternalism, emancipation, and slave-owning slaves in Africa and Brazil.

He is the author of Paternalism, Transgression and Slave Resistance in Brazil (De Gruyter Oldenbourg, 2022). His scholarship has received international recognition including the Kimberly S. Hanger Article Prize, an Honorable Mention from the Brazilian Studies Association (BRASA), and the Toni Bambara Article Prize.

From 2022 to 2024, he was a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Lisbon. He currently leads collaborative international research on women, slavery, Africa, and the African diaspora.
