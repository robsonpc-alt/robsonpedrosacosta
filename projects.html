Projects & networks

Collaborative research across the Atlantic

Research projects and international networks connecting scholars working on women, slavery, Africa, the diaspora, religion, and resistance.

Current funded research

Principal Investigator
2025–present
CNPq / Programa Conhecimento Brasil

Women of Africa, Women of the Diaspora: Connections, Transgressions and Resistance

International collaborative project connecting researchers in Brazil and abroad and investigating African and diasporic women through slavery, freedom, gender, intersectionality, family, motherhood, religion, resistance, mobility, and the slave trades.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions
2022–2024
University of Lisbon · Horizon Europe

SLAVESRELIGION – Slaves of Religion: Paternalism and Resistance, Brazil

Research on slavery in properties belonging to religious institutions, based partly on extensive archival research in Portugal. A second research phase continues from 2025.

International research networks

Co-Coordinator
2026–present

Women Slavery Research Network

International network promoting interdisciplinary, comparative, connected, and transnational research on women and girls subjected to slavery, compulsory labour, dependency, forced migration, and other forms of exploitation across multiple world regions.

Emerging network
2026–present

Studies on Women of Africa and the Diaspora

Initiative under development to connect researchers working on African and Afro-descendant women across Africa, Brazil, Latin America, the Caribbean, and the wider diaspora.

Public engagement & digital humanities

Conexões Atlânticas / Atlantic Connections

Digital public-history initiative dedicated to African and Afro-descendant history and culture through digital dissemination and public history.

Dona Isabel, que História é essa?

Public-history and science-communication initiative developed with Fundação Joaquim Nabuco, transforming research into freely accessible digital products.

History, Memory and Innovation

FACEPE-funded initiative connecting historical research, digital technologies, ethnic-racial education, heritage, and Black and traditional communities.

Malungo Lab

Laboratory of Digital Humanities and Social Innovation, coordinated 2021–2022, dedicated to public history, digital humanities, innovation, and educational experimentation.
